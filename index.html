tilt4d

Rendering as a projection from four dimensions to two.

An ordinary 3D renderer shows one flat slice of spacetime: everything as it is right now. Here the world is a single fixed four-dimensional scene, with the whole history of every object laid out at once, and you are a camera inside it. What you see is still a three-dimensional slice of it, but the slice need not be level.

Besides turning left and right and looking up and down, there is one more way to turn: tilting, which swings your line of sight out of space and into time. Ahead of you the slice then dips into the past, behind you it rises into the future, by one second for every C metres at a tilt of 45°. Your own time slows as you tilt: at a right angle it stands still, and beyond that it runs backwards. You move about as usual, but within the slice you see: walking forward in a tilted view takes you further back in time.

It resembles relativity, but it is not: the rotation is an ordinary Euclidean one and light is instantaneous, so the world stretches along the tilt instead of contracting.
